We have all heard the same formula repeated over and over: attend every lecture, chase a perfect GPA, get your degree, and everything else will fall into place automatically.

The reality on the ground in computer science and artificial intelligence is very different.

During our latest session at UdeM AI, we explored principles inspired by classic CS student survival guides (notably the philosophy of the well-known HUST CS guide) alongside our own firsthand experience. The goal: rethinking university not as an end in itself, but as a strategic environment where every hour invested must yield measurable impact.

Below are the 4 pillars for structuring your trajectory.

## 1. The Perfect GPA Is a Mirage (Reverse-Engineer Your Courses)

Deeply mastering a concept and maximizing a university exam score are two distinct skills. Exams primarily reward the ability to reverse-engineer the expectations of a specific grading format.

Chasing a 4.3 GPA at the expense of everything else often yields a poor cost-benefit ratio. What actually matters:

- **Define a critical threshold**: Hit the GPA floor required for your concrete targets (scholarships, graduate admissions).
- **Identify low-ROI courses**: Don't burn all-nighters on secondary courses at the expense of real technical projects.
- **Refuse the "good student" trap**: Universities are large institutions with their own incentives. Taking ownership of your future means deciding for yourself which durable engineering and research skills to build.

## 2. High-ROI Time Allocation: Align Effort with Your Target

Your time in undergrad or graduate studies is finite. Every scheduling decision should flow directly from your exit objective:

| Target | Priority Strategy | Trap to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industry / Big Tech | Targeted algorithmic prep (LeetCode), production deployments, software engineering internships. | Spending months on unapplied theory without a shipping code portfolio. |
| Frontier Research | Publishable projects, ML competitions, mastery of compute architectures. | Staying in a passive assistant role or overcommitting to non-technical club admin. |

Student clubs and extracurriculars are great early launchpads for building a network. However, learn to recognize when your involvement stops compounding your growth and becomes uncompensated busywork. Knowing when to pivot is part of the trajectory.

## 3. Undergraduate Research: Surface Prestige vs. Agency

When seeking a first research internship, the default instinct is to cold-email the most famous faculty names in the field. That is often a tactical mistake.

World-famous PIs manage massive labs and rarely have bandwidth for direct 1-on-1 mentorship with undergrads. By contrast, collaborating with an early-career professor or an accessible research scientist offers decisive advantages:

- **Direct mentorship**: Frequent technical feedback instead of one 10-minute check-in per month.
- **Methodological autonomy**: You work on core problem formulation and model architecture rather than menial data cleaning.
- **Faster publication cycles**: You learn firsthand how to run experiments, write papers, and submit to workshops or conferences.

Once your first paper is accepted and your execution is proven, doors to the largest labs open without friction. Technical substance always precedes surface prestige.

## 4. Where to Position Yourself in Machine Learning Today

Beyond foundational theory, three technical tracks stand out for their immediate impact and high demand:

1. **Model Quantization & Compression**: Making massive models run on constrained hardware without degradation. Mathematically rigorous with immediate practical value.
2. **Inference Serving & AI Infrastructure**: Optimizing compute clusters, orchestrating high-throughput inference, and designing hardware-aware pipelines. Engineers who bridge systems programming and AI runtimes are among the most sought-after in the industry.
3. **Robotics & Reinforcement Learning**: Grounding foundation models in physical environments and dynamic control is the next major engineering wave.

## The UdeM AI Vision: Transforming the Club into a Student Research Lab

To put this philosophy into practice, we are positioning UdeM AI as a direct incubator for technical research:

- **An outcome-driven student lab**: Moving beyond purely social events to form autonomous project squads capable of building and publishing.
- **Competitions as a publication vehicle**: Competing in international ML challenges (model safety, applied AI, robotics) and turning rigorous methodologies into workshop and conference papers.
- **Active engagement with the Montreal ecosystem**: Leveraging reading groups and frontier seminars across Montreal (including Mila) to engage in direct technical discussions with researchers.
